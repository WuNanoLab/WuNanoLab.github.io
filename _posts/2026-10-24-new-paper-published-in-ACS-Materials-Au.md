We are pleased to announce the publication of our research article, ["Size and Zinc Substitution Govern the MPI and MRI Performance of Ferrite Nanoparticle Tracers"](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsmaterialsau.6c00147), in ACS Materials Au. Dr. Kai Wu was selected as one of the "2026 Rising Stars in Materials Science" by ACS Materials Au. With this recognition, this research article was invited by the journal and is included as part of the ACS Materials Au special issue "2026 Rising Stars in Materials Science."

Magnetic nanoparticles that support both magnetic particle imaging (MPI) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) are attractive for dual-modal imaging, combining the quantitative, background-free tracer detection of MPI with the anatomical contrast of MRI. In this work, the team compared four lab-synthesized, quasi-cubic ferrite nanoparticle formulations: 30 nm and 10 nm Fe<sub>3</sub>O<sub>4</sub>, and 30 nm and 10 nm Zn<sub>0.4</sub>Fe<sub>2.6</sub>O<sub>4</sub>. Their morphology, physicochemical properties, cytocompatibility, and cellular uptake were systematically characterized. Using magnetic particle spectroscopy (MPS) as an MPI-relevant screening method, the 30 nm zinc ferrite produced the strongest harmonic signals in both water and agar phantoms, while agar embedding generally reduced harmonic amplitudes by restricting Brownian rotation. In T1-weighted MRI phantom imaging, the 10 nm nanoparticles generated well-localized hypointense contrast, whereas the 30 nm nanoparticles caused susceptibility-related signal loss extending beyond the inclusion boundaries. Overall, the study clarifies how core size and zinc substitution regulate the magnetic, biological, MPI, and MRI behavior of ferrite nanoparticles, and identifies 10 nm Zn<sub>0.4</sub>Fe<sub>2.6</sub>O<sub>4</sub> as a promising balanced tracer candidate for dual-modal MPI-MRI imaging.
