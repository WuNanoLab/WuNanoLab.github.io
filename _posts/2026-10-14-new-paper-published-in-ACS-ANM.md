We are pleased to announce the publication of our review paper, ["Magnetic Nanoparticle Transducers for Magnetogenetics: Materials Design, Biointerfaces, and Transduction Mechanisms"](https://doi.org/10.1021/acsanm.6c02937), in ACS Applied Nano Materials. Magnetic nanoparticles have evolved from passive imaging and delivery agents into active transducers that convert external magnetic fields into localized biological stimuli, opening new opportunities for magnetogenetics. This review examines three major transduction modalities: magnetothermal transduction, where nanoparticles heat under alternating magnetic fields to activate thermosensitive ion channels such as TRPV1 for wireless neuronal activation and deep brain stimulation; magnetomechanical transduction, where nanoparticles, nano/micro-discs, and nanochains generate forces and torques to activate mechanosensitive channels and integrin-mediated pathways; and magnetoelectric transduction, where magnetostrictive-piezoelectric materials produce local electric fields for neural stimulation, nerve regeneration, and wound healing. The review further discusses how particle geometry, anisotropy, surface chemistry, protein corona formation, and field waveform govern biological outcomes, outlines key design principles such as mechanism-specific dosimetry and geometry-field co-design, and highlights opportunities for programmable, closed-loop magnetic biointerfaces for precise control of cellular and tissue function.
